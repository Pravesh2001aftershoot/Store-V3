# Photographer Dashboard: PRD

## Summary

The client-facing side of Store V3 (the gallery, portfolio and print store) is built. Until now every setting was edited on the client page itself: the cover, heading and footer editors open from pencil buttons in the gallery, and the portfolio and store couldn't be edited at all. The Photographer Dashboard (`/dashboard`) moves that work into one place built for the photographer. It's laid out like a working dashboard: a sidebar on the left and four sections.

| Section | The photographer can… |
|---|---|
| **Galleries** | Create a gallery per client. In each one: add photos, sort them into collections, set the order and the cover, design the cover, grid, collection headings and footer against a live preview, choose who can open it and what visitors can do in it, share the link, and see who visited and what they downloaded, shared, favourited, commented on and bought. Each gallery picks the store catalog its clients buy from. |
| **Portfolio** | Edit their profile, portrait, intro, which galleries and categories appear, and the contact section. |
| **Website** | Build a one-page website at `/site` from ready-made sections — Hero, About, Portfolio (a few galleries, then a link to the full portfolio), Pricing, FAQ and Contact — against a live preview, the same way a gallery is designed. Reorder or hide sections and edit their text, photos, packages and questions. |
| **Store** | Create catalogs (for example Weddings, Newborns). In each one: show or hide products and packages, put them in order, rename them, set sizes, prices, option add-ons and package discounts, and build their own packages. |

Every change saves as it's made and shows on the client pages straight away. There is no draft or publish step, except in a gallery's Settings step, which has an explicit Save.

## Goals

1. **One place to set up a gallery.** Photographer tasks shouldn't live on client pages.
2. **See it as the client will.** Every design change is previewed on the real client page, at desktop, tablet or phone width.
3. **Reuse, don't rebuild.** The gallery's cover, heading and footer editors are reused as they are, so the dashboard and the gallery can't drift apart.
4. **Feel like the gallery.** The dashboard uses the same tokens, controls and motion, and stays calm and photo-first even with a lot of settings.
5. **Works on a phone.** A photographer should be able to fix a price or swap a cover from their phone.

## Non-goals (prototype)

- Accounts, sign-in, or a client list. A gallery's "client" is just a name on the gallery.
- A server. Settings live in this browser (see [Architecture.md](Architecture.md)).
- Actually delivering the Share step's email (**Send** is simulated in the prototype), a client list, order management and fulfilment.
- Real event tracking. Gallery analytics show sample data (see Gallery: Analytics).
- Delivering the website's enquiry form (it shows a thank-you but sends nothing), custom domains, more than one page, and free-form page building (the website is made of fixed section types).
- New categories, and products that aren't made from the client's photos (every product takes at least one gallery photo). Photographers can create products and packages, but only in the delivered categories.

## Users

**The photographer.** They deliver dozens of galleries a year and are comfortable with Lightroom-style photo managers. They want defaults that look good, and a fast way to make each gallery feel personal.

## Requirements

### Shell

- R1. A left sidebar with the logo, the four sections — Galleries, Portfolio, Website, Store (Galleries and Store show counts), the five most recent galleries, and the photographer's profile, which opens their portfolio. From 1024px it's 248px wide; from 640px it collapses to a 72px icon rail; on phones it's a drawer opened from a 56px top bar that names the section. Inside a gallery (`/dashboard/galleries/<id>`) the sidebar is hidden from 640px up so the gallery editor gets the full width; the step rail's **Back** button leads back. Phones keep the top bar.
- R2. Every page has its own URL: `/dashboard/galleries`, `/dashboard/galleries/<id>/<step>`, `/dashboard/portfolio` and `/dashboard/website` (`?part=` names the open part in both), `/dashboard/store` and `/dashboard/store/<catalog>`. The old `/dashboard/gallery/<step>` redirects to the sample gallery.
- R3. Each gallery has **View** (its client link), and each catalog has **Preview store** (the print store with that catalog), both in a new tab.

### Galleries

- G1. A grid of gallery cards, newest first: cover photo (or "No photos yet"), title, date, photo count and catalog. Search by title, client or date. A dashed **New gallery** card leads the grid.
- G2. **New gallery** (page header or card) asks for a title (required, up to 60 characters), client name (defaults to the title), date (defaults to this month), the store catalog, and whether to start empty or with the sample photos. Creating it opens its Photos step. A new gallery has one collection, "Highlights".
- G3. A card's menu: Edit gallery, Settings, View as client, **Publish** (or **Move to draft** once published), Delete. Inside a gallery only the Design step has a header, in the preview's toolbar: the image panel toggle on the left, Open (the client gallery) on the right. Other steps have no header bar. Title, client, date and catalog are edited in Design's Gallery info part (R10) or from the card's Settings.
- G4. **Settings** edits the title, client, date and catalog. Delete is there too, after a confirmation; it removes the gallery's photos (including uploads), design and link. The last gallery can't be deleted.
- G5. Each gallery has its own photos, collections, cover, grid, headings and footer. Clients open it at `/g/<id>`. The app opens on the dashboard (`/` redirects to `/dashboard`). An unknown link shows "This gallery isn't available".
- G7. **Publishing.** Every gallery starts as a **Draft**. Publishing it is the mandatory step before sharing: the Share step is locked until the gallery has been published once. Publish from the Design preview toolbar (**Publish** button), the Share step's empty state (**Publish gallery**) or the card menu. Once published, the toolbar shows a status pill (**Published** / **Draft**) whose menu has **Move to draft** or **Publish**. The gallery card shows a **Draft** or green **Published** tag, and so does the image panel beside the album name. Moving a published gallery back to draft does **not** lock sharing again: links, email and QR codes keep working and stay exactly the same (the first publish date is kept), and the Share step shows a note with **Publish again**. Status is a label only in the prototype: the client gallery at `/g/<id>` opens whatever the status (see Open questions).
- G6. The print store follows the gallery the client came from: its banner names that gallery, "Back to gallery" returns to it, and it sells from that gallery's catalog.

### Gallery: Photos (step 1)

- R4. Add photos with a button or by dropping files anywhere on the page. JPEG, PNG, WebP and AVIF are accepted; uploads are resized to 2400px on the long edge, with a 720px thumbnail. Files that can't be read are skipped and counted in the toast.
- R5. Collections: create, rename, reorder (move earlier or later) and delete. Deleting moves the collection's photos to another collection; photos are never deleted with it. At least one collection always remains. Names are up to 32 characters and must be unique.
- R6. Photos are selection-first. A tap selects, Shift-click selects a range, ⌘/Ctrl+A selects everything in view, and Esc clears. Bulk actions: Move to a collection, Make cover (one photo selected), and Remove from the gallery.
- R7. Removed photos go to a **Removed** view, where they can be restored. Uploaded photos can also be deleted for good from there, after a confirmation. Gallery photos can only be removed, never deleted.
- R8. Reorder by dragging (desktop), in a collection or in All photos (dropping on a photo in another collection moves them there), or with ⌥/Alt + arrow keys on a focused photo. Arrow keys move focus between photos. Drag photos onto a collection tab to move them. No reordering in Removed or while searching.
- R8a. Search photos by file name: case-insensitive, within the open view, with a no-matches state.
- R9a. On Design, an image panel, toggled from the left of the preview toolbar, shows the photos beside the preview: rename the album, Images / Videos tabs (videos not supported yet), each set collapsible with add photos, rename and delete, per-photo Move to / Remove / Set as cover, multi-select (check, ⌘/Ctrl- and Shift-click) with bulk Move to / Remove, drag to rearrange within or between sets (desktop and tablet; ⌥/Alt + ←/→ from the keyboard), and + New Set. Its Full view button opens the Photos step.
- R9. The Photos header has two actions: **Create Set** (primary) and **Add Images** (secondary). There is no Reset for the photo arrangement and no "Next: Design" button; steps are switched in the step rail.

### Gallery: Design (step 2)

- R10. Five parts: Gallery info (title, client, date, print store catalog, Delete; saves as you type), then Cover, Grid, Collection headings and Footer. On screens 960px and wider they're in their own icon rail on the right edge (the step rail is on the left); on narrower screens they're chips above the preview.
- R11. Picking a part opens its editor. On screens 960px and wider the parts are in the right-hand parts rail, the editor docks in a right-hand column beside the preview, and one editor is always open (Cover first, or the part in `?part=`), and the editor column can be hidden from the preview toolbar to see a wider preview; on narrower screens it opens as the gallery's bottom sheet over a full-width preview.
- R12. Cover, heading and footer editing use the gallery's own editors, with the same tabs, controls, limits and templates.
- R13. Grid sets the default layout clients start with: arrangement (masonry or justified rows), photo size and spacing. Clients can still change their own view.
- R14. The preview is the real client gallery. It scrolls to the part being edited, updates as settings change, and can render at desktop (1280), tablet (820) or phone (390) width, scaled to fit.

### Gallery: Settings (step 3)

Follows the Gallery Settings Revamp PRD (Confluence, GA space, 26 Nov 2026), plus the extra options this prototype already had (Personal use only, Web size edge and watermark, paid / free High res, Allow hiding in bulk).

- R14a. **Who can open the gallery:** *Anyone with the link* (one link and expiry date) or *By role*. By role has Client, Guest and Vendor roles, at least 2 of them, and either **one link for all roles** (checked by default; visitors pick their role; one expiry date) or one link per role, each with its own expiry date. Every link reads `gallery.aftershoot.com/` + gallery name + ending (`/view` for Anyone and the single link; `/client`, `/guest`, `/vendor` per role). Only the gallery name is editable (lowercase letters, numbers, hyphens; spaces become hyphens, other characters are dropped as you type; it can't be empty) and it's shared by all the gallery's links. It starts from the gallery title. Every link has Copy.
- R14b. Settings are per audience: the whole gallery, or each role (switched with a role control above the list). Each role keeps its own settings. They're grouped as **Access** (Sign-in method, Password protection, Face scan sign-in), **Engagement** (Filter by face, Favorites, Comments, Hide photos) and **Privacy** (Screenshot protection, Downloads, Sharing, Set visibility). Comments only exist with roles; Hide photos only on the Client role (photos a Client hides don't show to Guests or Vendors).
- R14h. **Sign-in method**, one at a time. Anyone with the link: **No sign-in needed** (default), **Collect email on sign-up** or **Approved clients only**. A role: **Password only** (no email, the role's password), **Collect email on sign-up** or **Approved [role]s only** (people added by name, email and optional phone).
- R14i. **Defaults for new galleries** (Revamp PRD section 5): Anyone: no sign-in, no password or face scan; Filter by face, Favorites, Downloads (Web size + High res, photos and videos, no PIN, no limit), Sharing (photos and videos) and Set visibility (all sets) on; Screenshot protection off. Client: Approved clients only, face scan on (Whole gallery), Comments and Hide photos on, Screenshot protection off, the rest as Anyone. Guest: Collect email on sign-up, face scan on (Whole gallery), Comments off, Screenshot protection on, the rest as Anyone. Vendor starts with the Guest defaults. One link for all roles is on.
- R14c. Options under a setting while it's on: a password (letters and numbers, 4–20); after a face scan, **Whole gallery** (tagged "Filter Face On") or **Only photos they're in** (tagged "Filter Face Off"), with an info tooltip on each and no label or sub-text; hiding in bulk; downloads (an optional auto-generated download PIN; **Personal use only**, off by default: "Before their first download, visitors agree to use photos for personal, non-commercial use only. They’re asked to contact you for commercial use.", which makes the client gallery's "Download photo" ask for that agreement once (root PRD G44); photo downloads with Web size / High res and a download limit, video downloads; the client gallery's Download panel applies the PIN, sizes and limit). With Web size picked: **Web size** (`Select`: 1,200px or 2,048px long edge, default 2,048px) and **Watermark web-size downloads** (off by default). **High res** (while it's picked): **Paid · from $9 a photo** (default; the lowest Digital Download price, without "from" when there's one file) or **Free**. Paid High res is sold as the store's **Digital Download** (and optionally **Whole Gallery Download**), priced in Store (R26); the choice shows only while this gallery's store catalog shows Digital Download, otherwise a note says "High res downloads are free. To charge for them, show Digital Download in this gallery's store catalog (Store)." While selling, the download limit counts free downloads only. The client gallery sells High res from a single photo, a selection, the whole gallery and the store (root PRD G45); photo and video sharing; which sets show in the gallery.
- R14d. Rules: one sign-in method per audience. **Password only** turns Password protection on and locks it. With **one link for all roles**, approved roles need no password; if two or more roles sign in without an approved list (Collect email or Password only), each of them needs Password protection with a different password; with only one such role, it doesn't. Face scan → Whole gallery switches Filter by face on and locks it; → Only photos they're in switches it off and locks it; turning face scan off gives the switch back. The **download limit** counts per visitor email, so it's disabled unless sign-in is Collect email or Approved, and changing to a sign-in without email clears it. Locked settings show their state with the reason.
- R14g. **The client gallery applies the saved settings.** Visitors meet a sign-in screen with only the steps that apply (pick a role on a shared role link, email or approved email, password, face scan) and an expired link stops them. Once in, they only see the actions their audience allows: favorites, hide (Client role; bulk with "Allow hiding in bulk"), filter by face, only their own photos after a face scan, sharing, set visibility, screenshot protection and downloads. Galleries whose settings were never saved, and the preview here, open without sign-in. Comments aren't built in the client gallery yet.
- R14e. A search field filters settings by name. Each group collapses and shows how many of its settings are on.
- R14f. Changes are a draft until **Save**. A bar at the bottom shows while there are unsaved changes, with **Dismiss** and **Save**. Save is stopped when roles are under 2, the gallery name is empty, a password isn't 4–20 letters or numbers, two roles on one link share a password, or photo downloads have no size: the bar shows the reason, the role with the problem opens and the field and its caption turn red. Otherwise Save opens a **Save summary** of every change. Changes that cut off people who already have the link (link or gallery name changes, a role removed, new gates such as email, approved list, password, face scan or download PIN, features or sizes turned off, sets hidden, people removed from an approved list) are listed in a red box with their effect, and the button reads **Save anyway**. Switching between Anyone with the link and one link for all roles keeps the link, so it isn't breaking.
- R14j. No favorites limit and no bulk share (Revamp PRD). "Allow hiding in bulk" is kept from the earlier prototype although the Revamp PRD drops it: confirm with product.

### Gallery: Share (step 4)

- R15a. **Before the first publish** the Share step is an empty state instead of the card: a rocket icon, "Publish your gallery to share it", "Once it’s published, you can send it by link, email or QR code. Your links stay the same even if you move it back to draft later." and **Publish gallery** (on phones in the bottom CTA bar). Publishing toasts "Gallery published. You can share it now" and shows the card. **Back in draft after publishing**, a note sits above the card: "This gallery is in draft. Your links, emails and QR codes stay the same, so anything you’ve already shared keeps its link." with **Publish again**.
- R15. Layout: one column, no page title: the **Share your gallery** card. How people get in (the card's **Links** tab, with the gallery's cover beside the links), from Settings: an access summary (linking to Settings), then each link with Copy and Open and its expiry (one shared link, or one per role), and per audience its **password** and **download PIN** with Copy. With roles, every role's password shows, since one gallery can have several.
- R15a. **Share your gallery** by **Links** (first, open to start), **Email** or **QR code** (a switch in the card's header). Email and QR code are one email and one QR card for everyone, whatever the access mode. There's no per-audience switch. Both point at the gallery link (`/g/<id>`); with roles, visitors pick theirs when it opens, even when each role also has its own link.
- R15b. **Email:** recipients (typed addresses, Enter or comma; invalid addresses stay in the field; with roles, each role's approved list can be added as a group with an **Add approved clients (N)** checkbox, ticked to start), a **Template** picker, Subject and Message (the message holds the greeting and sign-off; there's no separate sign-off field; empty uses default wording made from the gallery; both take variables, Gallery name, Client name, Client email and Expiry date, suggested as you type `{` and filled in the email), and **Include** checkboxes for the gallery link, password, download PIN and expiry date (only those that exist; with roles they read Passwords, Download PINs, Expiry dates and cover every role). With roles the email says which roles to choose from, then one line per role with what it needs (approved roles sign in with the email it was sent to, password, download PIN, its own expiry when each role has a link). Everyone on the email sees every role's details. The email is the message, then the included details. A live preview shows the email. **Send** sends it to everyone in To (simulated in the prototype). The wording and include choices are saved per gallery.
- R15b1. **Email templates.** The wording starts from a template picked above Subject. Built-in templates ship with the product: **Gallery ready** (the default wording), **Pick your favourites**, **Gallery expiring soon** and **Thank you**, each a subject and message with variables. Picking one fills Subject and Message, and the photographer can then tweak them for this gallery. **Save as new template** names the current subject and message (up to 40 characters, unique) and adds it under **Your templates** in the picker of every gallery (account-wide, up to 20). A saved template can be updated with **Save changes** after editing, or deleted; built-in templates can't be changed, only saved as a new one. Which template a gallery's wording came from is saved with its wording.
- R15c. **QR code:** a dismissible placement tip, then the cards as tiles side by side (label, code, caption, then the printed lines beside a download button). With **a link per role**, one card per role for that role's own link (the role name over the title, and only that role's password and download PIN), each downloaded on its own. Otherwise one card with the gallery title, the QR code for the gallery link, "Scan to open the gallery", the passwords (always printed) and optionally the download PINs (with roles, one line per role, then "Choose Client or Guest when it opens"). Download saves it as a PNG at 1080 × 1350. Links are copied from the links card, not from the QR panel.
- R17. **Email history** (built, hidden for now: `EmailHistory` isn't rendered; sends are still recorded): every email sent from this step, newest first, saved per gallery, in an accordion that starts closed. Each row shows the first recipient, "+ n more" (hover, focus or tap lists every recipient in a tooltip) and the date and time sent. Four rows show and the rest scroll. With nothing sent yet it shows an empty state.

### Gallery: Analytics (step 5)

- R17a. Totals for the gallery: **Visitors** (with how many gave an email and how many are anonymous), **Downloads** (single photos and full gallery), **Revenue** (orders and average order) and **Favourites** (with shares and comments).
- R17b. **How people download:** a bar splitting downloads into single photos and full gallery, with each side's download count and how many people did it.
- R17c. **People:** everyone who opened the gallery with an email, with their downloads, favourites, shares, comments, amount spent and when they were last active. The client is tagged "Client". Search by email or name; sort by Most recent, Most downloads, Most favourites, Most shares or Highest spend.
- R17d. Opening a person shows their activity by kind, down to the photo: **Downloads** (full gallery downloads with their size, then each photo), **Favourites**, **Shares** (with the channel: Link, Email, WhatsApp, Facebook), **Comments** (the text and the photo) and **Orders** (each item with its photo, size, quantity and price). Copy email and Email (mailto) are in the footer.
- R17e. **Export CSV** downloads the people list as shown (one row per person).
- R17f. The prototype shows sample activity, generated per gallery and stable between visits; the page says so. A gallery with no photos shows "No activity yet".

### Portfolio

- R18. Profile: portrait (upload, pick from portfolio photos, or use the default), name, role, location, availability, year started, email (validated), Instagram, and specialities (add or remove; at least one).
- R19. Intro: headline (defaults to "Hello! I'm <first name>."), bio, buttons on or off, and the facts strip on or off with each fact on or off.
- R20. Work: section title; categories (rename, reorder, hide, which also hides their galleries); galleries (search, filter by category, rename, hide). A hidden gallery's page shows "This gallery isn't available".
- R21. Contact: heading, which detail rows show, and the header's Enquire button on or off.
- R22. The profile also fills in the gallery footer's contact details.
- R23. Laid out like Design: a live `/portfolio` preview, the open part's editor docked to its right, and a parts rail on the far right (Profile, Intro, Work, Contact). The preview follows the open part. Below 960px the parts are chips above the preview and each opens its editor as a sheet.

### Website

- W1. The photographer's website is one static page at `/site`, made of six sections: **Hero**, **About**, **Portfolio**, **Pricing**, **FAQ** and **Contact**, under a sticky header (site title, links to the visible sections, **Enquire**) and above a footer (footer text, Portfolio link, Back to top).
- W2. **Site settings:** site title (defaults to the photographer's name, 40 max), footer text (defaults to "© <year> <name>", 80 max), the header's Enquire button on or off, and the section list: every section can be switched off; About, Portfolio, Pricing and FAQ can be moved up or down. Hero stays first and Contact last.
- W3. **Hero:** layout Full-bleed (photo fills ~86% of the screen, text over it) or Split (text beside the photo); headline (60), subline (160), a button on or off with its label (24) that goes to Contact; a landscape photo picked from the portfolio (defaults to the newest gallery's first landscape photo).
- W4. **About:** heading (defaults to "Hi, I’m <first name>."), story (600; defaults to the portfolio bio), a portrait-orientation photo from the portfolio or the profile portrait, and a quick-facts row (location, year started, availability from the profile) on or off.
- W5. **Portfolio:** heading, intro (200), button label (defaults to "View full portfolio"), 3 or 6 galleries, and up to that many featured galleries picked in order (none picked = the newest). Only galleries visible on the portfolio are offered. Cards open the gallery's portfolio page; the button opens `/portfolio`.
- W6. **Pricing:** heading, intro, currency ($, €, £), up to 4 packages (name 30, price, "per" 20, description 120, up to 8 inclusions of 60, and one "Most popular" package highlighted), reorder and remove (with Undo), and a small-print note (160). A package without a name isn't shown; with no packages the section isn't shown. Each card's button goes to Contact.
- W7. **FAQ:** heading and up to 12 questions (question 100, answer 400), reorder and remove (with Undo). A question without both parts isn't shown; with none the section isn't shown. The first answer starts open.
- W8. **Contact:** heading, intro, button label; an enquiry form (name, email, optional event date, message, validated inline) or, with the form off, an email button; and Email, Location and Instagram rows on or off. Details come from the portfolio profile.
- W9. Every text field shows its default as a placeholder; clearing it returns to the default. Each section has **Reset section**; Site settings has **Reset website**.
- W10. Same workspace as Design (R10): live `/site` preview, the open part's editor docked on its right, and a parts rail on the far right (Site settings, a divider, then the sections in page order). Below 960px the parts are chips above the preview and editors open as a bottom sheet.

### Store: catalogs

- C1. A list of catalogs as cards: name, product and package counts, the lowest price, four product thumbnails, which galleries use it, and a "Customised" tag once edited. The **Main catalog** always exists and can't be deleted; new galleries use it unless another is picked.
- C2. **New catalog** asks for a name (up to 40 characters) and how to start: **Everything** (every product and package on), **Nothing** (everything off, to switch on what you sell) or **A copy** of another catalog. Creating it opens it.
- C3. A card's menu: Edit catalog, Rename, Duplicate (a copy named "<name> copy"), Preview store, Delete. Deleting a catalog moves the galleries using it to the Main catalog, after a confirmation that says how many.
- C4. Catalogs are independent: a price, a hidden product, a product or a package in one never changes another. Copies get their own products and packages.

### Store: one catalog

- R24. Header: a back link to Catalogs, the catalog name (click to rename), which galleries sell from it (with links), Reset, and Preview store. Summary: products live, packages live, the price range and how many items are customised.
- R25. Products: filter by category; drag a row's handle to reorder (only when showing All); a visibility switch on every row; and an edited price shows with the original struck through.
- R26. Product editor tabs:
  - **Details:** show in store, name, tagline and badge. It warns when a hidden product is still sold inside a package.
  - **Sizes:** the price of each size, and whether it's offered.
  - **Options:** the add-on price of each choice, and whether it's offered.
  Every option keeps at least one choice. "Reset product" goes back to the catalog values.
- R26b. **Digital Downloads** category: Digital Download and Whole Gallery Download (one price, default $199, original files). Digital Download is sold as **file versions**, edited on its **Files** tab (instead of Sizes): each is a resolution (**Original resolution**, or a long edge of 6,000 / 5,000 / 4,000 / 3,600 / 3,000 / 2,800 / 2,400 / 2,048 / 1,600 / 1,200px), **Watermark** on or off, and a price. Defaults: Original resolution $15, 3,600px $12, 2,800px $9, none watermarked. **Add a file** adds the next unused resolution (up to 8); ✕ removes one (at least one stays); two identical files are flagged "Same as another file". Each file can also **Sell the whole gallery in this file**: a **Whole-gallery price** and a **Discount** slider (0–40% in 1% steps, like packages) with "Clients pay $119 ~~$149~~ · save $30 for every photo, including ones added later". Because an order switches to the whole gallery once photos one by one would cost more, an amber warning shows when the sale price would switch an order of 3 photos or fewer: "Cheaper than one photo ($9): buying any single photo will get the whole gallery for $5 instead." or "Clients buying just 3 photos would get the whole gallery for $25 instead of paying $27." It warns only; saving isn't blocked. Defaults: Original $199, 3,600px $149, 2,800px $99, no discount; a new file starts without one (ticking it suggests 10× the per-photo price). Clients choose the version in the gallery's Download photo and Download panel, Quick buy and the store. Whole Gallery Download's **Files** tab lists the versions it sells and their prices (read-only, "Set those prices and discounts in Digital Download → Files."); with none it isn't offered. Hiding Digital Download stops a gallery selling High res. Digital products can't go in packages, and New product isn't offered in this category (it creates the product in the first category instead).
- R26a. **New product** (beside the Products / Packages switch, and in an empty category) creates the photographer's own product in this catalog and opens it. It starts hidden, named "New product", in the category being shown (or Wall Art under All), with one 8 × 10" size at $50. It's tagged "Yours" in the list. Its editor has four tabs:
  - **Details:** show in store; Name (32), Tagline (80), Badge (16); Category (one of the store's four); Description (400) and up to 6 Highlights (60 each), shown under Details on the product page; Delete product (also removes it from the photographer's packages).
  - **Images:** up to 6 product images (resized to 1000px and stored in the browser). The first is the cover, used on the product card, the catalog row and the catalog card; the product page shows them all as thumbnails beside the "your photo" preview. **Look** picks which mockup style shows the client's photo in it (Loose print, Framed, Canvas, Metal / flat panel, Acrylic block, Book cover, Photo only), and a switch lets clients turn photos landscape.
  - **Sizes:** how photos work (One photo per item; A set of photos per item, 2–10; Album or book, with photos included, most photos and a price per extra photo), then up to 10 sizes, each width × height in inches (1–60, which sets the crop shape and the label) and a price. A product keeps at least one size.
  - **Options:** up to 4 options (like Finish or Colour), each with a name, how it's shown (Buttons, Cards with a note, Colour swatches) and up to 8 choices with a name, an add-on price and a colour or note. The first choice is the default. Choices without a name, and options without a named choice, don't show in the store.
  A custom product works everywhere a catalog product does: shop lists, the product page, the builder, the cart, and the photographer's own packages.
- R27. Packages: reorder, show or hide, name, tagline, Most popular, and a discount from 0–40% with the live package price, its value and the client's saving. The delivered packages' contents are read-only, and products hidden from the store are flagged.
- R27a. **New package** creates a package in this catalog (tagged "Yours") with one item, and opens it. Its contents are editable: up to 6 lines, each a product, a size and a quantity (1–50; books are always one), with the line's price. An empty package stays hidden from the store. It can be deleted from its Details tab.
- R28. Changes apply to the live store of every gallery using the catalog: lists, product pages, the builder, cart prices and package prices.

## Success criteria (prototype)

- A photographer can take the mock gallery from as delivered to customised (new photos, a new collection, a cover, a footer) without leaving `/dashboard`.
- A photographer can create a second gallery from their own photos, give it a Weddings catalog with different prices, a product and a package of their own, and its client sees those prices while the first gallery's client doesn't.
- Every edit shows in the preview in under a second, with no reload.
- A photographer can turn on a website with their packages, three FAQs and a featured set of galleries, and send a visitor from it to the full portfolio, without leaving `/dashboard`.
- Every tab is usable at 390px wide.
- On phones a page's primary action is always within thumb reach: it sits in a bar fixed to the bottom of the screen (New gallery, New catalog, New product / New package, Create Set + Add Images, Send, Save + Dismiss for settings). Two actions stack, primary on top.

## Open questions

- **What does Draft do for clients?** Galleries now have a Draft / Published status and sharing unlocks on the first publish (G7), but the status doesn't change what clients see: `/g/<id>` opens in either state, and edits still go live immediately. Open: should a draft gallery show clients "This gallery isn't available" (while keeping its links for when it's published again), and should edits to a published gallery wait for a re-publish?
- **Per-gallery or account-wide?** Now explicit: the portfolio, footer contact details and catalogs are photographer-wide; cover, headings, grid, photos and the choice of catalog are per gallery. Open: should a gallery be able to override a single price without a whole catalog?
- **Projects vs galleries.** A gallery is one client delivery. Some photographers will want a project (a wedding) holding several galleries (engagement, wedding day). Not modelled yet.
- **Catalog per client in a gallery?** Today one catalog per gallery. Schools and events may need different catalogs for different viewers of one gallery.
- **Analytics: what counts as a visitor?** Today a person is an email. Should guests without an email be tracked per device, and should the client see who else viewed their gallery?
- **Analytics date range.** All time only for now. Photographers may want last 7 / 30 days, or activity since the link was sent.
- **Settings vs Gallery info.** The gallery card's Settings opens a "Gallery settings" modal (title, client, date, catalog) and the rail's Settings step is also titled "Gallery settings" (access and permissions). Inside a gallery those fields are now Design's "Gallery info". Rename the modal to match, or rename the step (for example "Access")?
- **Email templates: account or gallery?** Saved email templates are account-wide today. Open: should the photographer be able to set their own default template, rename one, or share templates across a studio team?
- **Settings templates.** The source mockup also had saved templates of settings (Default, Wedding – Clients & Guests, Portrait – Public) and Free / Paid download sizes (paid downloads are now store products, see R14c and R26b). Templates aren't built yet: are they needed for v1? Should paid download revenue show in Analytics → Revenue and Orders?
- **Website: where do enquiries go?** The form validates and thanks the visitor but sends nothing. Production needs delivery to the photographer (email, an inbox in the dashboard, or a CRM), spam protection and a record of enquiries.
- **Website: one page or several?** Today it's one page with fixed section types. Do photographers need separate pages (a full About, a Pricing page per service), custom sections, their own domain, and should the website replace `/portfolio` as the public home?
- **Website pricing vs store packages.** Website packages are services (wedding coverage), separate from store packages (prints). Should they link, e.g. a collection that includes an album from the catalog?
- **Should clients still see edit pencils?** The gallery's own pencils remain for now, but are hidden in the dashboard preview. Once there's a photographer login they should only show to the photographer.
